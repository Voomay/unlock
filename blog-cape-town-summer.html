5 Unforgettable Things to Do in Cape Town This Summer (A Local Guide’s Picks)

Between November and March, Cape Town stays bright until after 8:00 PM. The Atlantic sparkles, the mountain trails smell of sun-warmed fynbos, and every pavement table from Sea Point to Kalk Bay fills up by late afternoon. Here is how to make the most of a Cape Town summer—without getting sandblasted by the afternoon wind or stuck in a two-hour queue.

Written by Louis Hendry Registered Western Cape Tourist Guide • WC 16800
Local Field Notes • Summer Edition

Quick Summer Cheat-Sheet

  • Best Months: Late November through March (warmest days, longest sunsets, and dry coastal weather).
  • The "Cape Doctor" Rule: When the southeasterly wind blows hard in the afternoon, skip open stretches like Blouberg and head to sheltered coves like Beta Beach (Bakoven), Clifton 1st–4th, or Windmill Beach.
  • Beat the Heat: Do Table Mountain or peninsula drives before 9:00 AM, or save the mountain for the late-afternoon sunset slot.

1. Swap Crowded Main Beaches for Beta Beach & Windmill Cove

Everyone knows Camps Bay and Clifton 4th, and on a calm 28°C morning they are glorious. But if you visit Cape Town in December, January, or February, you will quickly meet our famous southeasterly wind—affectionately called the "Cape Doctor" because locals swear it blows all the smog and cobwebs right out to sea.

When the wind picks up on wide beaches, walk five minutes south of Camps Bay into the quiet residential lanes of Bakoven to find Beta Beach. Tucked behind giant granite boulders with a dead-on view of Lion’s Head and the Twelve Apostles, the water here is flat, glass-clear, and sheltered from the gusts. Over on the False Bay side (where the Indian Ocean influence makes the water 4–5°C warmer), Windmill Beach just north of Boulders in Simon’s Town is another boulder-ringed cove where you will often spot an African Penguin swimming right past your towel.

Louis's Insider Tip Parking in Bakoven and Clifton is notoriously tight after 10:30 AM in peak summer. On our private tours, we drop you right at the footpath entrance and collect you when you're ready for lunch—zero parking stress.

2. Catch a Shaded Morning Walk or Sunday Concert at Kirstenbosch

Lying against the eastern slopes of Table Mountain, Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden is one of the few places in the world where a botanical garden sits inside a UNESCO World Heritage floral kingdom. In midsummer, when the city bowl feels hot at midday, the ancient yellowwood and camphor trees here keep the air cool and shaded.

Walk across the curved steel-and-timber "Boomslang" Centenary Tree Canopy Walkway, which snakes above the treetops with panoramic views toward Constantia, or hike partway up the shaded ravine of Skeleton Gorge. If you are in town on a Sunday afternoon between December and March, pack a picnic blanket and a bottle of Constantia Sauvignon Blanc for the Kirstenbosch Summer Sunset Concerts on the sloping lawns.

3. Take the Late-Afternoon Cable Car Up Table Mountain (Or Walk Kloof Corner)

The biggest mistake first-time visitors make is joining the Table Mountain Aerial Cableway queue at 10:30 AM under the midday sun. Instead, check the wind forecast the evening before and aim for either the first car up at 8:00 AM or the late-afternoon slot after 4:00 PM.

In late afternoon, the harsh overhead glare softens into warm amber light across Lion’s Head, the City Bowl, and Robben Island. If you want a short, rewarding 25-minute leg-stretcher without doing a full three-hour summit climb, walk the stone steps up Kloof Corner right off Tafelberg Road—it delivers one of the best sunset vantage points in the entire city.

4. Morning Tidal Pool Dips & Fresh Fish in Kalk Bay

Summer mornings along the False Bay coast have a rhythm all their own. Locals head down to the historic ocean-fed tidal pools at Dalebrook or St James (famous for its row of brightly painted Victorian bathing boxes) for a salt-water swim as the sun rises over the Hottentots Holland mountains across the bay.

After your swim, stroll five minutes into Kalk Bay for a flaky croissant at Olympia Café’s back-street bakery, browse the cobbled arcade boutiques and vinyl shops, and watch the brightly painted wooden trawlers unload their daily catch at Kalk Bay Harbour. Grab a paper parcel of fresh snoek or hake and chips right on the harbour wall while Cape fur seals bob in the water below.

5. Drive Chapman’s Peak at Golden Hour with a Sundowner Stop

Carved directly into the near-vertical red granite cliffs between Hout Bay and Noordhoek, Chapman’s Peak Drive (the M6) is 9 kilometres and 114 curves of pure coastal theatre. While most big tour coaches barrel through around midday, driving north-to-south or south-to-north in the final hour before sunset turns the whole Atlantic horizon molten gold.

Pull into one of the stone-walled picnic bays high above Hout Bay, watch the Atlantic rollers wrapping around the Sentinel peak, and toast the end of the day as the sky shifts from tangerine to deep indigo.
