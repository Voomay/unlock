Where to Hang Out in Stellenbosch & the Cape Winelands: Beyond the Big Tasting Rooms

Just 45 minutes east of Cape Town, granite mountain peaks rise sharply behind rows of Old Vine Chenin Blanc and Cabernet Sauvignon. Founded in 1679, Stellenbosch is South Africa’s second-oldest town—and while big commercial wine estates draw the tour buses, the real magic of the Winelands lives in its oak-shaded streets, boutique family cellars, and mountain-view verandas.

Written by Louis Hendry Registered Western Cape Tourist Guide • WC 16800
Winelands Insider Guide

Stellenbosch & Winelands At a Glance

  • Drive Time from Cape Town: 45–50 minutes via the N1 or N2 (combine Stellenbosch and Franschhoek in a single full-day loop over the scenic Helshoogte Pass).
  • Best Time to Visit: Year-round! Summer (Nov–March) brings harvest energy and outdoor lawn picnics; Autumn (April–May) turns the vineyards gold and crimson.
  • Golden Rule: Never try to squeeze more than 3 wine estates into one day—slow down, pair a morning cellar tasting with a long vineyard lunch, and leave time to stroll town on foot.

1. Morning Coffee & People-Watching on Church and Dorp Street

Before visiting a single vineyard, start your morning in the historic heart of Stellenbosch town. Unlike many wine regions that are purely rural, Stellenbosch is a vibrant university town lined with 300-year-old oak trees, whitewashed Cape Dutch architecture, and running water furrows (leiwater) along the pavements.

Grab an outdoor table along Church Street or Rynveld Street—local favourites like Meraki or De Warenmarkt (housed in an 18th-century historical building) serve flat whites and warm artisanal pastries while students, winemakers, and cyclists pass by. Afterwards, wander down Dorp Street and pop into Oom Samie se Winkel, an authentic Victorian trading store that has been operating since 1904.

2. Drive Over Helshoogte Pass for Boutique, High-Altitude Cellars

If you want to escape large commercial tasting rooms where you feel like a number at a counter, take the winding R310 up Helshoogte Pass—the mountain road linking Stellenbosch to Franschhoek.

Perched high on the slopes of the Simonsberg and Banghoek mountains, estates up here enjoy cooler breezes and breathtaking views back toward False Bay and Table Mountain in the distance. Places like Tokara (which also has a lovely delicatessen, olive oil tasting, and sculpture terrace), Delaire Graff, and tucked-away family farms in the Jonkershoek Valley let you taste single-vineyard Bordeaux blends and crisp Sauvignon Blancs while looking out over a sea of green vines.

Louis's Insider Tip South Africa has strict drink-driving laws (virtually zero tolerance). Having a private chauffeur-guide means everyone in your group can actually enjoy the reserve tastings and wine pairings without anyone drawing the short straw to drive!

3. Walk Through the Dylan Lewis Sculpture Garden

Even travellers who have visited Stellenbosch three times often miss its most extraordinary cultural hangout. Tucked against the foothills of the Stellenbosch Mountain in Paradyskloof is the Dylan Lewis Sculpture Garden.

Over seven hectares of former farmland were sculpted and replanted with indigenous fynbos, tranquil water features, and 4 kilometres of walking paths showcasing more than 60 monumental bronze sculptures—from prowling leopards and fragmented human forms to abstract works that weather naturally into the landscape. Visits are strictly by pre-booked appointment, meaning it never feels crowded.

4. Long Vineyard Lunches & Tree-Shaded Farm Picnics

In the Western Cape, lunch isn't a 30-minute pitstop—it's the centrepiece of the afternoon. Between Stellenbosch and nearby Franschhoek, you can choose between white-tablecloth tasting menus or barefoot picnics under ancient oak trees.

If you love relaxed outdoor dining, book a farm picnic basket at Boschendal or Spier, packed with freshly baked sourdough, local farm cheeses, charcuterie, snoek pâté, and summer salads. Prefer a long verandah lunch? Sit out on the terrace at an estate overlooking the Simonsberg crags with a chilled bottle of Cape Méthode Cap Classique (our traditional-method sparkling wine).

5. Late-Afternoon Wine Bars on Bird & Church Street

Most wine farm tasting rooms close their doors between 4:30 PM and 5:00 PM. That is when the action moves back into Stellenbosch’s pedestrian-friendly centre.

Pull up a stool at Bartinney Wine & Champagne Bar on Bird Street—a moody, candlelit hangout beloved by locals—or sit under the fairy-lit courtyard trees along Church Street to sample small-batch wines from independent Swartland and Stellenbosch winemakers who don’t have public tasting rooms of their own.
